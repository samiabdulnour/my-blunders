# The Source Corpus

An annotated, tiered bibliography for building the dictionary on real
scholarship. Verified 2026-07. Legal baseline: works published before 1931
are US public domain (PD); later works are presumed in copyright.

**Licensing red lines for contributors**

- **Freely ingest/quote:** Project Gutenberg PD texts, pre-1931
  archive.org scans, Wikisource.
- **Reuse with license:** Perseus (CC BY-NC-SA / BY-SA), DreamBank
  (CC BY-NC-SA, non-commercial), Wikipedia (CC BY-SA).
- **Citation-only** (model on it, cite it, quote briefly under fair use,
  never bulk-copy): ARAS, *The Book of Symbols*, all Tier-2 dictionaries,
  the Collected Works translations, all post-Jungians, Eliade / Campbell /
  Kerényi / Zimmer, ETCSL and Theoi page text.
- archive.org hosts both legal **controlled digital lending** ("borrow")
  records and unauthorized user uploads of in-copyright Jung volumes.
  **Link only borrow records — never the unauthorized full PDFs.**

### 1c. Accessible Jung (in copyright; CDL borrow links verified)

- ***Man and His Symbols*** (1964) — the front door for users; the bluntest
  anti-symbol-dictionary statements. [CDL](https://archive.org/details/manhissymbols00jung)
- ***Memories, Dreams, Reflections*** — Jung's own big dreams (the
  multi-storey house, the underground phallus); active imagination
  first-hand. [CDL](https://archive.org/details/memoriesdreamsre0000jung_o1c5)
- ***Dream Analysis: Seminar 1928–1930*** — the method *as performed*,
  week by week on a real dream series. [CDL](https://archive.org/details/dreamanalysisnot0000jung)
- ***Children's Dreams: Seminar 1936–1940*** (Philemon, 2008) — Jung's most
  didactic step-by-step procedure. Print/institutional only.
- ***The Red Book*** (Norton, 2009) — the active-imagination corpus; proof
  Jung treated even his own symbols as needing years of elaboration, never
  lookup. Cite; don't ingest.

## 3 · Symbol archives & encyclopedias

### Tier 1 — institutional / scholarly backbone

- **ARAS** ([aras.org](https://aras.org)) — the Archive for Research in
  Archetypal Symbolism: ~18,000 images with scholarly commentary,
  cross-indexed by symbol, culture, era. Grown from Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn's
  Eranos collection (Jung commissioned her iconographic research in 1934).
  Paid membership; **[ARAS Connections](https://aras.org/) journal is
  free**. Our best methodology model; citation-only.
  History: [Public Domain Review essay](https://publicdomainreview.org/essay/images-from-the-collective-unconscious/).
- ***The Book of Symbols*** (ARAS/Taschen 2010, ISBN 9783836514484) —
  ~350 essays, ~800 images, organized Creation & Cosmos → Plant World →
  Animal World → Human World → Spirit World. **The gold-standard entry
  anatomy** (see `SCHEMA.md`). In print, citation-only.
- **Thompson, *Motif-Index of Folk-Literature*** (Indiana UP 1955–58) —
  the master classification of narrative motifs (A–Z classes, stable IDs
  like B11 Dragon, F81 Descent to lower world). Open search interface:
  **[MOMFER](https://momfer.meertens.knaw.nl/)** (Meertens Institute) —
  WordNet-expanded semantic search, free. The 1955–58 text is in
  copyright: use the *methodology*, cite motif numbers freely.
- **ATU tale-type index** (Uther 2004) — cite type numbers freely
  (facts aren't copyrightable); don't reproduce descriptions.
  [Overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarne%E2%80%93Thompson%E2%80%93Uther_Index).
- **[ETCSL](https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/)** (Oxford) — ~400 Sumerian
  compositions with English translations, incl. *Inana's Descent*
  (c.1.4.1) and *Dumuzid's Dream* (c.1.4.3 — itself an ancient
  dream-interpretation text). Free to read; cite by catalogue number,
  quote short passages with attribution.
- **[Perseus](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/) / [Scaife](https://scaife.perseus.org/)**
  (Tufts) — the Greco-Roman corpus with canonical citations. **Actually
  licensed for reuse** (CC BY-NC-SA / BY-SA) — the primary quotable source
  for classical passages.

### Tier 3 — open corpora for myth citations (where amplification quotes come from)

| Resource | Scope | License / use |
|---|---|---|
| [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org) | Frazer's *Golden Bough* ([abridged #3623](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3623) and the pre-1916, PD 3rd-ed. volumes), Bulfinch ([#3327](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3327)), Grimm, Lang, the Eddas… | **US PD — fully quotable and ingestible.** Our bulk source |
| [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org) | Proofread PD primary texts: Eddas, Ovid, *Kalevala* (Crawford), Budge's *Book of the Dead* | PD / CC BY-SA — quotable, stable URLs |
| [sacred-texts.com](https://sacred-texts.com) | 1,700+ books of scripture, mythology, folklore | Underlying PD texts quotable (verify pre-1931); cite ISTA as location |
| [Theoi Project](https://www.theoi.com) | 1,500+ Greek-myth pages: summary + stacked primary-source quotes with exact citations | **The evidence pattern to imitate**: claim on top of block-quoted primary text. Take the citations, pull PD translations ourselves |
| [Internet Archive](https://archive.org) | PD scans + controlled digital lending | PD scans quotable; lending copies citation-only |
| [Encyclopedia Mythica](https://pantheon.org) | 11,000+ short articles | Orientation only — never a terminal citation |

**Reliability tiering for citations:** primary texts (Perseus / Gutenberg /
ETCSL / Wikisource) > scholarly secondary (Eliade, Kerényi, ARAS-style) >
Britannica > Wikipedia (orientation + its footnotes) > mythology fan sites
(never).

### Comparative-religion secondary (citation-only; CDL lending exists for most)

- **Mircea Eliade** — *Patterns in Comparative Religion* (its chapter
  structure — sky, sun, moon, water, stones, earth, vegetation, sacred
  space/time — is itself a symbol taxonomy worth emulating), *Shamanism*,
  *The Sacred and the Profane*
- **Joseph Campbell** — *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*, *The Masks of
  God* (rights actively managed by JCF — strictly citation-only)
- **Karl Kerényi** — *The Gods of the Greeks*, *Essays on a Science of
  Mythology* (with Jung — the theoretical charter for amplification)
- **Heinrich Zimmer** — *Myths and Symbols in Indian Art and
  Civilization*, *The King and the Corpse* — the essential Indian
  counterweight

## 4 · Dream-research datasets (the empirical counterweight)

- **[DreamBank](https://dreambank.net)** (Schneider & Domhoff, UCSC) —
  20,000+ real dream reports, searchable, **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** — the one
  true open dataset; usable for per-symbol frequency footers ("appears in
  n% of sampled reports")
- **Hall/Van de Castle coding system** — complete coding rules free at
  [dreams.ucsc.edu](https://dreams.ucsc.edu/): characters, interactions,
  emotions, settings, objects, with norms. The quantitative-empirical
  contrast to Jungian reading — we adopt a simplified HvdC facet block for
  *description*, never for *interpretation*
- **[SDDb](https://sleepanddreamdatabase.org)** (Kelly Bulkeley) —
  44,500+ reports with demographic tools; no blanket open license —
  contact before data reuse

## 5 · How a finished entry uses all of this

Stable motif-style ID + name/etymology → 2–3 sentence phenomenology of the
image *in dreams* → tradition-keyed amplifications, each backed by a
quotable PD primary passage (Perseus / Gutenberg / ETCSL / Wikisource
link) with a divergence note → opposites/shadow paragraph → Jungian
commentary citing CW ¶ and secondary literature → cross-references
(related motifs, Thompson/ATU numbers) → sources split into *quoted
(public domain, license noted)* vs *cited (in copyright)* → optional
DreamBank frequency footer.
